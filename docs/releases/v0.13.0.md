# JBeam Forge 0.13.0: workspaces, JBeam editing, part mods, Skin studio, importers, undo and a first-run tour

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.13.0.exe**: the installer. It updates an installed 0.11 in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.13.0-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.

0.12.0 was never published on its own, so this release also brings everything from it (further down). Coming from 0.11:
- The materials and objects packs are no longer inside the download: get them from the app (Downloads → Textures / Meshes).
- Projects are now format v19: 0.13 opens 0.11 projects, but 0.11 can't open projects saved by 0.13.
- Rescan your library once (Settings → Library) so body panels, suspensions and engines from your game list their options.

## New

**A tour on first start**
- The first time the app opens, a short tour runs on a practice car: it highlights one button at a time with a tip beside it and walks through the scene, sorting meshes into parts, materials, nodes and beams, the JBeam workspace, testing, moving parts, triggers and export. Skip it at any step; replay it from the home screen (Learn).
- **Help centre** (the ? button, Shift+F1): guides with worked examples, from your first mod to extensions.

**Workspaces**: tabs for Modelling, Materials, JBeam, Moving parts, Triggers, Scripts and Testing, each laid out for its job.

**JBeam workspace**
- Tables of nodes, beams and triangles: exact positions, weights and any jbeam property, typed in; hand-set values win over the part's preset on export.
- Name nodes logically by part and side in one go; move, scale, weigh and set properties on many at once; mirror to the other side; make triangles from picked nodes.
- Checks for short and long beams, nodes with too few beams, overlaps and flipped triangles, each shown in the viewport.
- Advanced mode (Settings → JBeam) shows every property, with the checks' limits and naming style adjustable.

**Moving parts and Triggers**
- Moving parts: doors, bonnets, boots, steering wheels and needles in one list with presets and previews.
- Triggers: place clickable boxes on the car, size and mirror them, and wire them to actions (open a door, a light, a script's key).

**Your model stays live**: when you save the model again in Blender, the app reloads it and keeps your parts, materials and structure (per mod, and Settings → Files).

**Skin studio** (Materials workspace): skins made easy.
- **Unwrap for skins**: the body panels are laid out like a skin template, seen from the left, the top, the right, the front and the rear. Every panel sits where it is on the car at one scale, so a stripe on the side view runs straight across the doors. Seams follow the views, and lone triangles are tidied so curved panels stay whole. The inside of a door shares its outside's place; flipped faces still land on the right side.
- Picks the body panels for you (not glass, lights, wheels or the interior), with a live preview by part or by stretch, and options: how far curved panels lean to the sides, one design for both sides, a full underside band.
- **Templates**: a PNG (2048 to 8192) and a layered SVG for Inkscape, Photoshop or GIMP: panels in colour and outlined, part names, each view titled with the way the car faces, the centre line and a one-metre bar.
- **See it on the car**: the template, or any skin, shown in the viewport.
- **New skin from a painted template**: your image becomes a paint design players pick in the game. Materials the panels share with other parts are copied when laying out, so a skin never lands where it shouldn't.

**DDS textures**: textures go into the mod as DDS with mipmaps (BC1, BC3 or BC5 for normal maps), with a size limit (per mod, and Settings → Export).

**More kinds of mod** (New mod)
- **Engine**: a changed engine from a car in the game, added to that car's parts menu.
- **Tyres**: universal tyres with your sizes, tread, pressures and grip, for every car whose rims take them.
- **Wheels**: universal rims (diameter, width, lugs, offset) for every car with that hub.
- **Body panel**: a new hood, bumper, door, fender, spoiler… for a car in the game. Pick the car and the panel from your install, bring in the stock panel as a guide to line up against, import your model: the mod adds it to that car's parts menu on the stock part's physics, so it attaches, bends and breaks like the original.

**Preferences like Blender's**: themes and accents, density and font size, navigation and editing options, and a keymap editor.

**Extensions**
- Extensions can read folders you pick for them (zips too), import models, start mods and sort meshes into parts.
- Examples come with the app (Settings → Extensions): **Forge toolbox**, a **BeamNG vehicle importer** (a car from the game or a mod: models, nodes, beams and triangles with their values, and every jbeam and configuration file for reference) and a **Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 importer** (proof of concept, from models exported with AssetStudio or AssetRipper).
- Their commands are also on the home screen. docs/extensions.md walks through writing your first one.

**Ported mods**: a mod brought over from another game says so. Tick that you own the game and that the mod is free (the Inspector with nothing picked, or the importer asks). The export waits for it, and the mod credits the game in its description and a `ported_from.txt`.

**Undo, one action at a time**: Ctrl+Z takes back every action on its own, and an action that does several things at once (fitting a suspension or engine, a paint scheme, bringing over a car) is one step. Quick ticks of a box are separate steps. Choose how many steps are kept in Settings → Editing (1 to 10000) and how many paint strokes (1 to 100). Ctrl+Z works wherever focus is; in a text field it undoes the typing first.

**Assetto Corsa import**: pick which model (detailed, LODs, collider, driver), sort into parts or not, and choose how effect meshes, hidden objects and paint are handled (also in Settings → Files).

## Also in this release: everything from 0.12.0

### New

**Vehicle scripts** (a new Scripts tab)
- 16 ready-made functions: wipers, power windows (frameless glass drop), folding mirrors, sunroof, convertible roof, power seats, pop-up headlights, welcome and ambient lights, active rear wing, launch control, pops and bangs, shift light, wind and road noise, emergency brake flash, and an Android Auto / CarPlay style head unit on your screen mesh.
- Set them up from a form, pick the meshes they move, choose their keys.
- A Lua editor that checks as you type and explains mistakes, with completions and help for BeamNG's vehicle API.
- A test runner: drive a scenario, press keys, see every value the script writes, and play its animations on the car.
- Save scripts to your library, share them, and download more (Downloads → Scripts).

**Extensions**: add your own commands, script templates and project edits (Settings → Extensions, docs/extensions.md).

**Configurations manager**: every configuration as a card with its picture and figures. Pick the default, reorder, import and save .pc files, compare two side by side, and fill in the vehicle selector details.

**Vehicle selector pictures**: studio pictures like the game's own, for the model and each configuration (size, angle and backdrop in Settings → Export).

**Drive shafts**: the gearbox now reaches axles from other cars. Rear, front or all-wheel drive, with a front/rear split and centre differential for all-wheel drive.

**Downloads and Settings**
- **Downloads** (toolbar, home screen, Ctrl+Shift+D): update the app to the latest GitHub release, or roll back to any older one. Textures and meshes each download from their own repository: everything, only what changed, or single items; any earlier version too.
- **Settings**, reorganised into sections: window size presets (720p to 4K, or remembered), start maximised or full screen, interface scale, render scale, anti-aliasing, frame rate cap, FPS counter, grid, reflections, viewport background, camera field of view, orbit and zoom speed, units (km/h or mph, hp/kW/PS, Nm or lb-ft), autosave, undo steps, recent projects, reopen the last project, export options, content folder, update checks and the download repositories.

**Engine, gearbox and driveline builders**
- **Engine:** drag the dyno curve, stretch the rev range, scale the weight, and change any number the engine has (limiter, inertia, turbo, backfire…).
- **Gearbox:** edit, add or space the ratios, with road speed per gear.
- **Driveline:** each axle's differential: open, LSD, viscous or locked, final drive, preload and locking.
- **More engines:** fit several; each configuration picks one, and players can swap in the parts menu.
- **The game's other parts:** for a suspension's or engine's slots (brakes, steering racks, turbos, ECUs…). Pick which is fitted, and which ship as in-game choices.

**Engine sound**
- Any of the game's engine sounds for intake and exhaust.
- A rev preview with the game's own recordings: play it, drag the revs and throttle, or "Rev it".

**Paints and painting**
- Factory paints and the car's three paint slots, per configuration too. Paint materials preview the way the game mixes them.
- **Paint studio:** brush, fill, patterns (stripes, fades, checks, camo, flake), text and image stamps, eyedropper, mirror both sides, and image round trips.
- **Vinyl editor:** layers of shapes, text and images, livery-editor style: gradients, cut-outs and clipping, groups, mirrored copies, 43 shapes and 10 ready-made designs, and `.jbvinyl` files to reuse on other cars.
- **Material brush:** drag carbon fibre, Kevlar, chrome, Alcantara and more onto the car. The painted triangles export as that real material.
- **Two-sided materials:** a different material on the inside.

**Materials and textures**
- **Channel views:** roughness, metallic, AO, normals and a UV checker.
- **UV tools:** a UV layout preview, and box or planar projection for meshes without good UVs.
- **Ambient occlusion baking** from the car's own shape, into a real texture.
- **Suggestions from the game's own materials** for "Use a BeamNG material".

**Hinges and the sandbox**
- Hinge every door, hood and trunk in one click.
- Sandbox scenarios: **Swing** (open to the stop and shut), **Wrench off** (should tear at the hinges), and **Drop on its suspension** (bottoming out, sag, pitch, bounce).

**Animated parts and cameras**
- The steering wheel, needles (revs, speed, fuel, temperature), pedals and handbrake move in game. Try each in the viewport first.
- Interior cameras: driver, passenger and hood. Look through them in the app.

### Fixed

- Hinged doors swung past their stop: the opening limiter was anchored too close to the hinge line to go taut. It now anchors where it works.
- Install detection with a trailing backslash in BeamNG's ini.
