# JBeam Forge 0.16.0: inside BeamNG on F10, every game setting in reach, AI mode, and a download library

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.16.0.exe**: the installer. It updates any earlier version in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.16.0-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.
- **jbeam-forge_0.16.0_amd64.deb**: Linux (Ubuntu, Mint, Pop!_OS…): `sudo apt install ./jbeam-forge_0.16.0_amd64.deb`.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.16.0.AppImage**: Linux, any distribution: make it executable and run it.
- **jbeam_forge_blender.py**: the Blender add-on (Blender 3.6 or newer). Install it from Edit → Preferences → Add-ons → Install.
- **jbeam_forge_ingame.zip**: JBeam Forge for inside the game, if you'd rather drop it in your mods folder by hand. The app installs and updates it for you (Settings → BeamNG.drive).

Projects are now format v24. 0.16 opens every older project; 0.15 can't open a project saved by 0.16.

## What the game said, and what it measured

- **What the game said** (Export, Test results, or the command palette) reads BeamNG's log after you've spawned the car and lists what went wrong with it, first causes first: missing parts, nodes, meshes, materials and variables, each with a button that jumps to the part in the editor.
- **Measure** (JBeam Forge in the game) puts the car through the game's own performance tests on its test map: 0-100 km/h, top speed, braking and off-road. The figures show in the game's vehicle selector straight away, and the next export writes them into the mod so they go wherever it goes.
- Each project remembers the BeamNG version it was made for, and says so when you open it after a game update, since that's when mods most often break.
- Export warns when another mod in your mods folder has a car folder with the same name. The game merges the two, and a car can come apart at spawn without anything looking wrong.

## Blender add-on

A small add-on for Blender (in the sidebar, N): pick a part type, position and variant and it names the selected meshes the way JBeam Forge reads them (`mycar_door_FL`, `mycar_bumper_race_F`), with extra pieces of a part numbered so they stay one part. **Export for JBeam Forge** writes the model; import that file once and, from then on, every save in Blender updates the car in JBeam Forge with your parts kept.

## Engine figures

The engine builder has **Set the figures (advanced)**: type the power, torque and rev limit you want, and the curve is reshaped to match.

## Structure, checked against the game's own cars

Each part's Structure section has a report card that compares its node weights, bracing, beam lengths and collision against the game's own cars, and says in plain words what to change when one is out of range.

## JBeam Forge inside BeamNG.drive

Press **F10** while driving and Forge opens over the game, the way the World Editor does on F11. Press F10 again to drive on.

- **The car I'm driving** opens that car as a project with its current configuration: every part, node and beam as the game built it, its models and its materials.
- **On the car** draws your project's structure on the car you're in.
- **Drive it** installs your mod as it is now and spawns it, in the game's own physics.

Install it from Settings → BeamNG.drive → JBeam Forge in the game. It comes with the app and keeps itself at the same version.

## Every setting, and your own versions of parts

- Every setting of a fitted engine or gearbox has a game-pad button: press it to make that value adjustable in the game's tuning menu, between a lowest and highest you choose.
- **Make a version** of any part of a fitted engine or gearbox: a race radiator with a bigger core and more coolant, a sport oil pan, a short-ratio gearbox. Each has its own name, price and values and shows up in the parts menu next to the original.
- Cooling, oil, fuel and brake settings now have proper names and ranges. **Advanced mode** (Settings → General) lists every number the game's parts have.
- Axles have a **Brakes & diff** page: brake torque, handbrake, brake response and cooling, tyre pressure and ABS, as well as the differentials.

## AI mode

Optional, and works with any AI you already use. Pick jobs (names and descriptions, prices, weights, structure, material looks, hinges and handles, scripts, configurations, vehicle details, engine and gearbox tune, tuning options), copy the request into ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Claude or anything else, and paste the answer back. Every change is checked and listed before you apply it, and a round is one undo step. **Iterate** asks again with your feedback.

No account and nothing to sign in to. Settings → AI mode can send requests for you with your own key, or to a free model on your own PC (Ollama, LM Studio).

## Downloads in one library

Materials, meshes and scripts now come from one public library that rebuilds itself whenever something is added. The app finds it without any setup. If you look after content, Settings → Downloads → Publishing adds materials, scripts and finished folders and publishes them from inside the app.

## Linux

JBeam Forge now runs on Linux too, from the same code as the Windows version. It finds BeamNG.drive whether you play the native Linux build or the Windows one through Steam's Proton (Steam from the package, Flatpak or Snap), and installs JBeam Forge in the game into whichever you play. Updates work the same way: the app offers the new AppImage or .deb.

Also on Windows: a moved BeamNG user folder (the game's "user folder" option) is now found by itself.

## Help

A **Reference** section in the Help centre covers every part of the app, each page from the basics to the advanced settings.

**Tutorials**: every tutorial in the app, step by step, in the order a mod comes together (getting started, building the car, moving parts and controls, engine and wheels, vehicle scripts, finishing and sharing, part mods, going further). The interactive ones start from Help and open the right workspace, and every vehicle script has its own.

## Fixed

Tested by exporting the demo car with dozens of the game's suspensions, engines and gearboxes, every vehicle script and every extra, and spawning each one in BeamNG.drive:

- Borrowed suspension parts whose own nodes all sit at one height (the Autobello's tie rods, several lower arms and limit straps) stretched or failed to load ("VY node not found"). They now also take the new car's body, the way they took a body node on their own car.
- A borrowed suspension, engine or gearbox could bolt onto whatever part of your car was nearest, including the doors, the hood or an engine block, and pull the car apart as it settled. They now bolt only to the body and its structure (frame, floor, subframes and the like).
- Where your body had no node close to a borrowed suspension's mounting point, several mounting points were pulled onto the same body node, the arms lost their shape and the car came apart. Each mounting point now stays where the suspension needs it and is bolted to the body around it (never to another borrowed part: a gearbox bolted to the engine's exhaust pulled it off).
- The second of two doors that share a node prefix (the front right, usually) was exported without its latch, so it hung open. Both doors now latch.
- Parts tucked against each other (a grille behind the bumper, headlights in their housings) pushed themselves apart when the car spawned, breaking their mounts and stretching their meshes. Nodes that start against another part's surface now stay out of self-collision; everything else still collides.
- A gearbox from one car fitted to another car's engine could tie its stiffest beams to a tiny exhaust node, and the car shook itself apart at spawn. Parts now attach to the nearest node heavy enough to hold them.
- A suspension that brings its own wheel (the Covet three-wheeler's tractor wheel) now brings that wheel along, instead of pointing at a part the mod didn't have.
- A tandem rear axle (the dump truck's) with a front suspension was refused as if it covered both ends. It's now allowed.
- An electric motor with a gearbox made for a combustion engine (one with a clutch) is now stopped at export with an explanation; the car couldn't start.
- Exported cars now have a drive controller and fuel tank or battery of their own, so they drive with any borrowed engine.
- Closing a side panel no longer makes the other panels grow.
- F10 in the game now opens JBeam Forge reliably, and says so on screen if something in the game is blocking it.

- Changing the game folder while the game's parts were being read lost the change; the new folder is now read afterwards.
- Parts made with the game's own materials wrote material names the game couldn't find; they're now written and defined as the game expects.
- Copy buttons failed when the window wasn't focused.
- Meshes of a car from the game bind to the nodes the game binds them to (a door's skin on the door panel's nodes), so they no longer come loose.
